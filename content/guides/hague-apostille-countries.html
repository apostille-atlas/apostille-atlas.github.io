An apostille is the one-step certificate used between countries that are party to the 1961 Hague Convention Abolishing the Requirement of Legalisation for Foreign Public Documents. Countries outside the Convention usually need a certificate of authentication instead, followed by embassy or consular legalization.

How to check

The authoritative list is the HCCH status table. As of its June 30, 2026 update it lists 130 contracting parties. The United States has been a party since October 15, 1981.

State offices handle both

Most state offices issue both apostilles and authentication certificates, so you tell them the destination country on your cover sheet or request form. Some states differ. In Georgia, for example, the GSCCCA issues apostilles while the Georgia Secretary of State issues the "Great Seal Certification" for non-Hague countries.
