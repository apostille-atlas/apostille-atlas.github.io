Teaching abroad, applying to a foreign university or getting a work visa often means apostilling your diploma or transcript. Unlike a birth certificate, a school record usually can't go straight to the state: most states want a school official's signature notarized first, and each state words that rule differently. This page lists each covered state's official rules.

Which office apostilles a diploma or transcript?

The apostille office of the state where the school is, after the document is notarized or certified as that state requires. Georgia, for example, apostilles transcripts only from Georgia schools (GSCCCA), and Arizona tells you to send documents from another state to that state (Arizona SOS). Not sure whether your document is state or federal? See state or federal: which office? Each state's address and fee are in the directory.

Does my college or university diploma need to be notarized?

In most covered states, yes, or it needs an official's signature that a notary has notarized. These are the rules each office publishes for school records:

Can a notary just certify a copy of my diploma?

Only in some states. Georgia allows it: a Georgia notary certifies the photocopy as a "true and exact copy of the original" and the apostille is attached to that copy (GSCCCA). Texas says a notary may make a certified copy of a diploma (Texas SOS). Illinois says the opposite: a notary cannot certify copies, and diplomas and transcripts must carry a school official's original signature and be notarized (Illinois SOS). Washington, a state we don't cover yet, accepts an original diploma without notarization, or notarized copies (Washington SOS). Many people keep the original: Washington advises against sending an original high school diploma because schools typically won't recreate it (Washington SOS).

Why do offices reject school documents?

Illinois lists "school diplomas and transcripts not originally signed and notarized" among its common reasons for rejection (Illinois SOS). North Carolina won't accept a registrar's school seal in place of notarization (NC SOS). Ohio rejects documents notarized by a notary from another state or country (Ohio SOS), and Pennsylvania wants originals, not photocopies (PA DOS). In New York, skipping the County Clerk step means the Department of State can't apostille it (NY DOS).

How do I get a diploma or transcript apostilled?

  1. Ask your school's registrar for an official transcript or diploma copy prepared for an apostille. Many registrars sign in front of a notary on request; check your state's rule in the table above.
  2. Check the notarization. North Carolina, Illinois and Ohio require a notary of their own state (NC SOS, Illinois SOS, Ohio SOS), and Pennsylvania wants the notarial certificate in English (PA DOS).
  3. Add any county step. New York requires a County Clerk certification of the notary before the Department of State (NY DOS).
  4. Send it to the state apostille office with its form or cover letter naming the destination country, the fee and return shipping. Applying from another country? See getting an apostille while living abroad. Short on time? See same-day and walk-in apostille offices.
