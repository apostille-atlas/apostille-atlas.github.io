A U.S. power of attorney used abroad is almost always a private document signed before a notary. The apostille comes from the state where that notary is commissioned, even if you live somewhere else. It is not a federal apostille unless the instrument somehow bears a federal official's signature. New York's Department of State names power of attorney on its how-to page, and Georgia's GSCCCA lists powers of attorney among Georgia notarized documents it will apostille.

Which office apostilles a power of attorney?

The apostille office of the state where the notary is commissioned. That matches the broader rule on state or federal: which office?: documents notarized by a state-commissioned notary go to that state, while FBI checks and other federal records go to the U.S. Department of State. Georgia will not apostille out-of-state documents, and Ohio rejects documents notarized by a notary from another state or country. Each state's address, payment and timing are in the directory; fees alone are in apostille cost by state.

How do I prepare a power of attorney for an apostille?

  1. Sign before a notary commissioned in the state that will issue the apostille. The California Secretary of State does not provide notarial services; get the notarization done first (California SOS FAQ). Texas says only the signer may make statements in a power of attorney; a notary cannot make those statements for the signer (Texas SOS).
  2. Send the original notarization, not a photocopy. California, Florida, Pennsylvania and Arizona all say a photocopy is not acceptable. New Jersey wants the original inked signatures of the New Jersey notary and of the person who signed before the notary.
  3. Add New York's County Clerk step if the notary is a New York notary. Notarized documents must be certified by the County Clerk of the county where the notary is commissioned before the Department of State will apostille them (NY DOS).
  4. Mail or walk in to that state's apostille office with its form or cover letter naming the destination country, the fee and return shipping. Applying from another country? See getting an apostille while living abroad.

Will an online (RON) notarization work?

Only check a state that publishes a rule. Texas says online (RON) notarizations can be apostilled if you include the notary's ledger details and a notarial certificate that states the document was notarized by electronic or online notarization by two-way audio and visual communication, and it warns that some countries still reject RON documents (Texas SOS). Other covered states' RON rules are not summarized here; that is a separate backlog guide. Do not assume another state matches Texas.

What if the power of attorney is for a lawyer in Italy?

The Italian consulates in New York and San Francisco publish citizenship-by-descent document lists, but they do not publish rules for a power of attorney given to a lawyer in Italy. Ask the lawyer what format they need. On the U.S. side, the apostille still comes from the state where the notary is commissioned.
