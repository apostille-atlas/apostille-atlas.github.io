An apostille itself is cheap: every office we cover charges $25 or less per document. What you pay depends on where your document comes from, how many documents you send, and a few extra fees some states add. Every fee below is copied from the issuing office's own official page.

What is the cheapest state to get an apostille?

Among the states we cover, Illinois charges the least ($2 per apostille), followed by Georgia and Arizona ($3 each) and Ohio ($5). New Jersey ($25) and California ($20) charge the most. You usually can't shop around, though: the apostille has to come from the state where the document was issued or notarized. New Jersey, for example, says it cannot apostille vital records from other states and that out-of-state records must be authenticated in the state of origin (NJ DORES). How much does a federal apostille cost?

Federal documents, such as an FBI background check, are apostilled by the U.S. Department of State, not by a state. Its fee is $20 per document (U.S. Department of State). See the U.S. Department of State guide and the FBI background check apostille guide for the steps.

Is the apostille fee charged per page or per document?

Per document. Texas and Pennsylvania both state that their $15 fee is per document, not per page (Texas SOS, PA DOS), and Illinois says each document needs its own apostille (Illinois SOS). So three documents from Texas cost 3 × $15 = $45 in state fees, plus your postage. New Jersey adds one detail: if you attach a notarized translation to a vital record, it issues two apostilles and charges two fees (NJ DORES).

What extra official fees can apply?

  • California: in person, a $6.00 special handling fee for each different public official's signature, on top of $20.00 per apostille (California SOS).
  • Florida: $20.00 instead of $10.00 per document when the document was certified by a Florida county Clerk of the Court (Florida DOS).
  • Arizona: a $25 in-person expedite fee (limit 6 documents), and an $18 notarial capacity certificate for documents going to Puerto Rico or a U.S. territory (Arizona SOS).
  • New Jersey: requests are paid online, and a convenience fee may apply on top of the $25 statutory fee (NJ DORES, NJ fee schedule).

Are apostilles cheaper for adoptions?

In some states, yes. Texas charges $10.00 per apostille for adoption proceedings, up to $100.00 per child (Texas SOS). New Jersey charges $5 per certificate obtained in connection with the adoption of a child (NJ fee schedule). North Carolina charges $10.00 for an adoption-related original and $5.00 for each duplicate original ordered at the same time (NC SOS).

What does the apostille fee not include?

  • Postage both ways. You pay to send the document, and most offices need a return envelope from you. California asks for a self-addressed return envelope (California SOS); New York returns documents by first class mail unless you include a prepaid shipping label for overnight delivery (NY DOS).
  • Getting the document ready. Many documents must first be a certified copy or be notarized, and some need a county-level certification before the state will apostille them. New York, for example, requires a County Clerk certification for locally issued birth, death and marriage certificates and for notarized documents (NY DOS). Those offices set their own fees, which are not covered here. See the birth certificate apostille guide.
  • Rejections. A request sent with the wrong fee or document comes back and costs you another round of postage. Ohio asks for the exact fee (Ohio SOS). North Carolina says its $10 fee may be refunded on request if a document is rejected and not processed (NC SOS).

How do I pay the apostille fee?

Each office sets its own rules. Florida accepts only checks or money orders, with no cash or credit cards (Florida DOS); New Jersey takes payment online by credit card or e-check (NJ DORES); Ohio accepts cards on mailed requests only with its Credit Card Authorization form (Ohio SOS). Check your state's page in the directory for exact payment details before you send anything.

Is it cheaper to do it yourself or use a service?

Doing it yourself is always cheaper, because a service adds its own fee to the same government fee. A service can still be worth it if you live abroad or are on a tight deadline; compare both in DIY vs paid apostille services, and for urgent cases see same-day and walk-in apostille offices.
