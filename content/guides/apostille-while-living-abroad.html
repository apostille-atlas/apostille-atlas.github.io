You can get a U.S. apostille without flying home: every state office we cover, and the U.S. Department of State, accepts requests by mail. The hard parts from abroad are paying (most offices want a check or money order from a U.S. bank) and getting the document back (most want a prepaid return envelope or label, and some won't ship outside the U.S.). This page lists each office's official rules so you can plan around them.

Can American apostilles be applied for remotely, from outside the United States?

Yes, by mail. The apostille has to come from the office where the document was issued or notarized: the state for state documents, and the U.S. Department of State for federal ones like FBI background checks. Not sure which applies? See state or federal: which office? Every office in our directory takes mailed requests, and New Jersey has you order and pay online first, then mail or drop off the documents with the confirmation page (NJ DORES).

How do I pay a state apostille fee from another country?

This is where most requests from abroad get stuck. Texas and Florida require checks or money orders drawn on a U.S. bank (Texas SOS, Florida DOS), and Ohio says it does not accept foreign checks or money orders (Ohio SOS). Pennsylvania is more flexible: a check from a foreign bank is accepted if it is in U.S. dollars and has a routing number (PA DOS). A few offices let you pay by card, which is easier from abroad: New Jersey takes credit card or e-check online (NJ DORES), Georgia has a credit card pre-payment voucher for mailed requests (GSCCCA), Ohio accepts cards on a mailed request with its Credit Card Authorization form (Ohio SOS), and New York's request form takes card payments with the CVV (NY DOS). The U.S. Department of State takes only a check or money order by mail (U.S. Department of State).

Can the office mail my apostilled document to an address outside the U.S.?

Most offices don't say whether they will mail abroad, so check your office's page or call before you count on it. North Carolina is explicit: courier packages (UPS, FedEx, DHL) will not be delivered outside the United States, you should give a U.S. return address, and documents sent without a prepaid return envelope go back by regular mail to a U.S. address (NC SOS). North Carolina also lets you have documents sent on to a third party: put that on your cover letter and address the prepaid envelope to them (NC SOS). Other offices return documents by regular mail when you don't send prepaid shipping: California by regular USPS mail (California SOS) and New York by first-class mail (NY DOS).

Return labels have rules too. Florida, Arizona and Pennsylvania (for UPS) want your own name and address as both sender and recipient on the label (Florida DOS, Arizona SOS, PA DOS). Texas and Georgia reject handwritten air bills (Texas SOS, GSCCCA); New York and Texas won't accept "bill sender" labels (NY DOS, Texas SOS); and Pennsylvania does not accept FedEx or DHL for returns (PA DOS).

How do I get a state-level apostille while living abroad?

  1. Get the right original. Order a certified copy from the issuing agency, or get the document notarized, as your state requires. Offices reject photocopies; California and Florida both say so (California SOS, Florida DOS). Some states need an extra step first: New York needs a County Clerk certification on locally issued vital records (NY DOS). The birth certificate apostille guide covers the common cases.
  2. Watch out for notarizing abroad. Ohio rejects documents notarized by a notary from another state or country (Ohio SOS). Georgia likewise says it cannot apostille out-of-state documents (GSCCCA). If you sign a document abroad, ask the receiving authority how it wants it certified before you rely on a U.S. apostille.
  3. Fill in the office's request form or cover letter and name the destination country. Texas and Ohio reject requests for use inside the U.S. (Texas SOS, Ohio SOS).
  4. Arrange payment the office accepts (see above).
  5. Include return shipping that meets the office's rules, or have it returned to someone in the U.S. who can forward it to you.
  6. Mail it with tracking. Pennsylvania recommends tracking on both the outgoing and return envelopes (PA DOS).

How to apostille an NYC marriage certificate without going to NYC?

By mail. New York's Department of State accepts mailed requests at PO Box 22001, Albany, NY 12201-2001 for $10 per document, but a locally issued marriage certificate must first be certified by the County Clerk of the county where it was issued (NY DOS). Details are on the New York apostille page.
