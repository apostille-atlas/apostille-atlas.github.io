A marriage certificate is apostilled by the state where the marriage was recorded. Washington's Secretary of State puts it plainly: vital records, including marriage certificates, must be apostilled by the issuing state. You need a certified copy from the office that keeps the record, and in several states someone specific has to sign it before the state will attach the apostille.

Who has to sign a marriage certificate before it can be apostilled?

What if I married in one state and live in another?

Use the state where the marriage was recorded. Washington's FAQ says that for a vital record issued in another state, you must get the apostille in that state, and New Jersey says it cannot apostille other states' vital records. Most offices accept requests by mail; see getting an apostille while living abroad if you are outside the U.S.

Do I need a translation too?

That depends on the destination country, so check its consulate; our apostille and certified translation guide shows what Spain, Italy and Germany ask for. If you do attach a notarized translation, New Jersey says it will issue two apostilles and charge two fees.
