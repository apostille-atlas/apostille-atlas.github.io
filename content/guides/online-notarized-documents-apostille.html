A document notarized online (remote online notarization, or RON) can only be apostilled by the state that commissioned the notary, and states treat these documents very differently. Some apostille them, one only as a printout with an extra certificate, and some refuse them outright. Check the official rule of the notary's state before you book an online session.

Can a remotely notarized document be apostilled?

Yes, in some states. An apostille certifies the signature of an official in the issuing state, so a RON document goes to the Secretary of State (or equivalent) of the state where the notary is commissioned, not the state you live in. Virginia, for example, says documents notarized in a different state must be authenticated by that state, and Delaware will not apostille documents notarized by out-of-state notaries (Virginia, Delaware).

What should I check before booking an online notary?

  • The notary's commissioning state. That state issues the apostille. If it refuses electronic notarizations (Virginia, Delaware), an online session there will not get you an apostille.
  • Paper or electronic. Kentucky works with the PDF itself; Oregon needs a printout with the notary's special-circumstances certificate; Texas wants the ledger details and the online-notarization wording on the certificate.
  • The receiving country or agency. Texas warns that some countries do not accept online notarized documents even with an apostille, so ask the recipient first. For country rules see which countries accept apostilles.

Which documents does this matter for?

Only documents that need a notary: powers of attorney, affidavits, consents and notarized copies. Certified vital records and court records are apostilled on the issuing office's certified copy, not on a notarization. See the power of attorney apostille guide, the birth certificate guide, and the apostille FAQ.
