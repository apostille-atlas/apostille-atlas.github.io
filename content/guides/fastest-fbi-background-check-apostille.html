Getting an apostilled FBI background check has two waits: the FBI check itself, then the federal apostille from the U.S. Department of State. States cannot apostille it (see state or federal: which office?). Here is how to make each step as short as the official rules allow.

What is the fastest way to get the FBI check itself?

Submit your request online. The FBI calls this "the fastest option" and says online requests come back "within 48 hours of visiting a United States Post Office location for electronic fingerprints," or in approximately 10 days after it receives a mailed fingerprint card. Mail requests take up to 15 days plus mail time. You must complete the application and payment online before going to the Post Office, and additional fees may apply there (FBI).

  • Fee: $18 per person, the same online or by mail. "You cannot pay extra for faster service" (FBI FAQs).
  • Ready for the apostille: the FBI says it authenticates all results with the FBI watermark and the signature of a division official at the time of submission, and you can send those to the Department of State for an apostille. It also says "requests to authenticate previously processed results will not be accepted" (FBI FAQs).
  • Don't ask the FBI to forward it: for mail requests, the FBI says not to send a return envelope or a request to forward your results to the Department of State for the apostille.
  • Channelers: FBI-approved channelers are private companies that submit the request for you, for an extra fee. The FBI says to contact each one for its own processing times, and that they can only serve U.S. citizens or permanent residents located in the U.S.

How does the walk-in drop-off work?

Bring Form DS-4194, your FBI check, the fee and a self-addressed prepaid envelope to 600 19th Street NW, Washington, DC 20006. Drop-off and pick-up run 7:30 to 9:00 a.m., Monday through Thursday, with no appointment needed. Someone else can drop off and pick up for you if their name or company name is written on the receipt at drop-off; photo ID is required at pick-up. In person, the office takes credit or debit cards and contactless payments such as Apple Pay, but not cash, checks or money orders (official page).

What if I have to mail it?

Use USPS with tracking to send Form DS-4194, the document, the fee and a self-addressed prepaid USPS or UPS return envelope to: Office of Authentications, U.S. Department of State, 44132 Mercure Circle, PO Box 1206, Sterling, VA 20166-1206. Pay by check or money order to the U.S. Department of State, with your name and address printed in the upper left corner and a check number over 100. To ask about status, use the Department's Contact Us form; it says it replies within 7 business days and asks you not to send a second request while you wait.

Can I get a same-day apostille in an emergency?

Only if you must travel abroad within 2 weeks because an immediate family member outside the U.S. has died, is dying or in hospice care, or has a life-threatening illness or injury. Email CA-PPT-TO-AUT@state.gov to ask for an appointment (10:00 a.m. to 2:30 p.m., Monday through Thursday). Bring proof of travel, such as a ticket or itinerary, and proof of the emergency, such as a death certificate or a doctor's signed hospital letter. If approved, the Department processes the request the same day.

Mistakes that cost time

  • Don't notarize the FBI check: the Department of State says a notarized original is no longer valid (preparation rules).
  • Write the destination country on Form DS-4194.
  • Don't send it to your state's Secretary of State; it will come back unapostilled. Our FBI background check apostille guide covers the standard steps.
