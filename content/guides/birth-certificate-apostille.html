A birth certificate is apostilled by the state where it was issued, not the state where you live now. You need a certified copy; photocopies and notarized photocopies are generally rejected. Many rejections come from the prep step, which differs from state to state:

StateWhat the official page says
New YorkLocally issued records must first be certified by the County Clerk. For NYC records, request a "Letter of Exemplification" when you order the record.
TexasCertified records must have been issued within the past five years.
GeorgiaCertified copies should preferably have been issued since 2000; the GSCCCA recommends getting a new copy if yours is older.
New JerseyRecords from 1923 onward come from the NJ Department of Health; earlier records come from the State Archives.
PennsylvaniaDeath certificates must be signed by the state registrar, not a local registrar.

For all other details (fee, address, timing), open your state's page in the directory.
