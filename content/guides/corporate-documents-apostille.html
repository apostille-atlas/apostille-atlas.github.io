Articles of incorporation or organization, certificates of formation, and certificates of good standing (also called certificates of status or of fact) are apostilled by the state where the company is registered. The apostille goes on a certified copy or certificate issued by that state's business filing office, not on a photocopy and not on a notarized copy. Several states have you order the certified copy and the apostille in one request.

How do I get my business entity documents apostilled?

Ask the business filing office in the state where the corporation or LLC was formed (or registered) for a certified copy of the filing or a certificate of good standing, and tell it the document needs an apostille and which country it is going to. Washington's Secretary of State puts it plainly: documents on file with its Corporations Division need certified copies or certificates from that division; send a written request with the entity name, UBI number, the destination country and your contact details. Corporation and LLC documents needing apostilles cannot be ordered online there, and because they come from two departments, both fees apply.

A company's records can only be apostilled by its home state. Delaware's Division of Corporations, for example, will not apostille out-of-state documents, so a Delaware LLC's certificate of good standing goes through Delaware, wherever the owners live.

Can I use a certificate of good standing I already have?

Sometimes. Florida accepts an original certified copy or certificate of status its office issued earlier (never a photocopy). Washington does not accept previously issued documents that have already left its office. If the receiving country wants a recent certificate, ask it how recent before you order.

What about bylaws, board resolutions and other corporate papers?

Documents the state never filed, like bylaws, minutes, resolutions or contracts, have no state certified copy, so they are usually signed in front of a notary first and the state apostille then covers the notary's signature. Texas lists copies of bylaws, contracts and business documents as non-recordable documents a notary may copy-certify, with a notarized statement from the issuer summarizing the contents. Georgia lists corporate documents among notarized documents it apostilles once notarized under Georgia law. Use a notary in the state whose apostille you will request; see the power of attorney apostille guide for the same notarize-then-apostille steps.

Is a corporate apostille state or federal?

State, for records from a state business filing office or a state notary. Documents signed by a federal agency go to the U.S. Department of State instead; see state or federal: which office?.
